# Declined the owner's proposed fan-interest goal; kept the second base goal

- **Sim date:** 2024-03-07
- **Ledger:** seq 1 (ruled free)
- **Staff input:** none. No advisors are engaged yet and the warehouse does not
  exist, so this was a GM call made on the owner's stated terms plus league-wide
  financial data from a probe save. **That is a real limitation of this record** —
  there is no dissent to weigh later because there was nobody to dissent.

## Decision

John Henry offered to replace **"Upgrade a position — Second Base"** (Average
priority, target 2024) with **"Improve your fan interest"** (High priority,
4-year duration). Declined, keeping the second base goal.

Declined rather than cancelled: declining risks locking the second base goal in
permanently, which is the outcome I want.

## Why

**The proposed goal conflicts with the goal he already made High priority.**
Building the farm to Top 6 by 2026 means trading major-league talent for prospects
and giving at-bats to unproven players — both of which suppress near-term fan
interest. Under [ADR 0015](../../docs/decisions/0015-gm-is-employed-not-appointed.md)
owner goals are the only scorecard, so accepting would have guaranteed failing one
of two High-priority goals across the same window.

**Fan interest is a consequence, not a lever.** Boston sits at 69 against a league
average of 64.4, with fan *loyalty* already at 8 of 10. The loyalty is there; the
interest is depressed by three mediocre seasons (.400, .481, .481). The reliable
way to move it is to win, which the .500 goal and the 2029 playoff goal already
grade me on. Accepting adds grading risk without adding a decision I can make.

**The duration is exactly wrong.** Four years at High priority spans 2024–2027 —
the build window, when interest is most likely to fall.

**What I would have surrendered is good.** Second base is specific, satisfiable in
one acquisition, aligned with the .500 goal, and only Average priority. Trading an
easy aligned goal for a hard conflicting one is bad before priorities are weighed
at all.

The counter-argument I rejected: interest drives attendance, which drives gate and
merchandising revenue, which drives budget. True, but I do not control the budget
([ADR 0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-staff-is-the-information-channel.md) notes
the owner does), his fiscal personality is Normal, and the causal chain runs
through winning regardless. It is the same lever with more steps.

## What I expect

- **Second base is addressed during 2024** and the goal is marked satisfied.
- **Fan interest does not rise materially on its own during the build.** I expect
  it flat-to-down from 69 across 2024–2026 while the farm goal progresses, then
  rising as the club gets good. If interest climbs substantially while we are
  building and losing, my central premise was wrong.
- The declined goal does not reappear.

## What would make this a mistake

- **Fan interest turns out to have levers I overlooked** — ticket pricing,
  marketing or facility spend that moves it independently of wins. Then I declined
  a goal I could have executed directly.
- **The second base goal proves trivial or moot** — the incumbent is fine, the goal
  self-satisfies in April, and I locked in something worthless instead of taking a
  four-year goal that would have shaped real decisions.
- **Declining costs goodwill.** Patience is *Demanding* and mood is currently
  *Ecstatic*. If declining measurably moves either, the option was more expensive
  than its stated terms suggested.
